# 001 — Rules and engine boundaries

Use independent Rust domain code and two long-lived KataGo analysis-protocol processes. The human worker requests `humanPolicy` with rank-conditioned metadata and `ignorePreRootHistory=false`, then samples proportional to legal probability. Its regular strong model searches at least 64 visits to judge passing; the strong preferred board move is never substituted for human sampling. Human-policy passes are excluded unless the strong model prefers passing. The separate strong worker has no human model and always requests BLACK perspective.

After a human pass, use at least 256 visits and ownership to recognize a settled position (occupied points have at least 0.8 absolute ownership confidence; score standard deviation is at most 2 points). If the preferred move still performs cleanup, explicitly search a forced pass at the same budget. Accept the pass only with at least 128 visits to that child and losses of at most 0.5 points and 1 percentage point of win probability, normalized for the opponent's color. The root itself consumes visits, so requiring the child to receive the entire root budget is incorrect. These conservative endgame heuristics address equivalent dead-stone cleanup without introducing a move limit or ending unfinished games from win probability alone. Two passes still enter explicit stone-status agreement; they do not finalize automatically. Reproduced and validated on the reported 326-move game on 2026-10-28.

Assessed Goban 0.20.1 source on 2026-10-27: its README advertises AGA, but released `rules/mod.rs` defines only CHINESE/JAPANESE and `check_super_ko` stores boards without side-to-move. Its positional superko cannot implement AGA situational superko. Isolate a small flood-fill rules engine with exact board+next-player history; passing is exempt from repetition rejection. Use AGA area scoring (stones plus surrounded empty regions, white komi 7.5), so pass stones and last-white-pass bookkeeping for equivalent territory counting are unnecessary. Dead groups require whole-group agreement; ambiguous status resumes play. No automatic dead-stone removal from uncertain ownership.

SQLite holds immutable node records separately from mutable game metadata and the active head. Finalization and the unique rating event share one transaction. Imported finished records do not count as training games. Review adds nodes without changing active continuation or result. Evaluations are normalized to Black before comparison; loss compares the pre-move preferred candidate with the actual candidate or the analyzed continuation.
